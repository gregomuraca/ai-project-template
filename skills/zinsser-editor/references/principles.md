# Principles from *On Writing Well*

This reference translates the book's chapter-level ideas into operational editing guidance. It paraphrases the ideas rather than reproducing the book.

## Part I — Principles

### 1. The Transaction
Writing is an exchange between a writer and a reader. Personality, judgment, curiosity, and integrity are part of the work.

**Agent rule:** improve communication without erasing authorship.

### 2. Simplicity
Complex subjects do not require unnecessarily complicated sentences.

**Agent rule:** prefer the simplest construction that preserves the full meaning.

### 3. Clutter
Prose accumulates redundancy, euphemism, jargon, qualifiers, and inflated phrasing.

**Agent rule:** remove language that performs no semantic, rhythmic, or rhetorical work.

### 4. Style
Style is not decoration added after the fact. Trying to sound impressive often makes prose less natural.

**Agent rule:** never upgrade plain language merely to sound literary, technical, or professional.

### 5. The Audience
Over-optimizing for an imagined audience can weaken the writer's conviction and voice.

**Agent rule:** adapt terminology, context, and detail to the reader without replacing the author's stance.

### 6. Words
Word choice includes meaning, specificity, connotation, sound, rhythm, and register.

**Agent rule:** choose the exact word, not merely a plausible synonym.

### 7. Usage
Good usage requires judgment. Language changes, and correctness depends partly on context and register.

**Agent rule:** distinguish genuine errors from informal, emerging, or contextually appropriate usage.

## Part II — Methods

### 8. Unity
A piece needs coherence in central idea, scope, point of view, tense, tone, and attitude.

**Agent rule:** establish these choices before line editing.

### 9. The Lead and the Ending
The beginning must earn continued attention. The ending should feel earned rather than mechanically appended.

**Agent rule:** test the opening for forward pull and the ending for natural closure.

### 10. Bits & Pieces
Good prose depends on sentence mechanics: verbs, modifiers, punctuation, paragraphing, revision, sound, and rhythm.

**Agent rule:** apply sentence-level diagnostics only after structural issues are understood.

## Part III — Forms

### 11. Nonfiction as Literature
Factual writing still benefits from selection, structure, narrative, detail, rhythm, and craft.

**Agent rule:** do not reduce nonfiction editing to grammatical cleanup.

### 12. Writing About People: The Interview
People become vivid through specific detail, distinctive speech, experience, and contradiction.

**Agent rule:** preserve revealing language and do not homogenize a speaker's voice.

### 13. Writing About Places: The Travel Article
Specific observation is stronger than generic description.

**Agent rule:** prefer observed detail over stock adjectives and travel clichés.

### 14. Writing About Yourself: The Memoir
Selective, specific memories often carry more meaning than exhaustive chronology.

**Agent rule:** favor meaningful detail and do not manufacture lessons the writer did not express.

### 15. Science and Technology
Technical subjects can be explained clearly without sacrificing correctness.

**Agent rule:** simplify the explanation, not the truth.

### 16. Business Writing: Writing in Your Job
Institutional prose often hides actors and actions behind abstraction, jargon, and passive constructions.

**Agent rule:** recover concrete actors, actions, decisions, and outcomes.

### 17. Sports
Specialized fields accumulate predictable clichés that can replace real observation.

**Agent rule:** detect domain-specific clichés, not only general ones.

### 18. Writing About the Arts: Critics and Columnists
Useful criticism connects judgment to standards, evidence, and reasoning.

**Agent rule:** prefer claim → criterion → evidence → reasoning over unsupported adjectives.

### 19. Humor
Humor often depends on observation, incongruity, timing, exaggeration, understatement, and specificity.

**Agent rule:** preserve existing comic logic; do not inject jokes by default.

## Part IV — Attitudes

### 20. The Sound of Your Voice
Recognizable voice emerges through vocabulary, cadence, formality, humor, certainty, and point of view.

**Agent rule:** treat voice preservation as an editing constraint.

### 21. Enjoyment, Fear and Confidence
Interest and curiosity create energy; artificial enthusiasm does not.

**Agent rule:** surface what is genuinely interesting instead of adding hype.

### 22. The Tyranny of the Final Product
Premature focus on polished output can prevent discovery of the right scope, structure, and meaning.

**Agent rule:** understand and organize before polishing.

### 23. A Writer's Decisions
Writing consists of repeated decisions about inclusion, omission, emphasis, sequence, explanation, and stopping.

**Agent rule:** at each section ask what the reader knows now, needs next, and why the section exists.

### 24. Writing Family History and Memoir
Authentic voice, selective memory, and specific detail matter more than exhaustive coverage.

**Agent rule:** optimize for authenticity and significance, not completeness.

### 25. Write as Well as You Can
Good writing depends on standards: clarity, accuracy, integrity, revision, and respect for the reader.

**Agent rule:** ask what remains weaker than necessary while resisting needless intervention.

## Condensed operating model

Use seven systems:

1. **Intent**
2. **Unity**
3. **Clarity**
4. **Anti-clutter**
5. **Structure**
6. **Voice**
7. **Craft**

Prioritize structural and semantic problems over cosmetic polish.
