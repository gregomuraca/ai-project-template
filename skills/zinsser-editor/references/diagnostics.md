# Diagnostics

Use this reference when the core skill identifies a likely sentence-, paragraph-, or voice-level problem.

## Intent and unity

Ask:

- Can the central point be stated in one sentence?
- Is the scope stable?
- Does point of view shift without a reason?
- Does tense shift without a reason?
- Is the tone appropriate to the purpose?
- Does each paragraph belong to the same piece?

## Clutter

Look for:

- duplicated meaning;
- throat-clearing;
- unnecessary qualifiers;
- inflated phrases;
- nominalizations hiding simple verbs;
- jargon where plain language is more precise;
- generic transitions;
- repeated conclusions;
- adjectives and adverbs compensating for weak nouns or verbs.

Do not delete language merely because it is optional. Optional words may carry rhythm, emphasis, humor, or voice.

## Precision

Prefer:

- named actors when agency matters;
- concrete nouns over broad abstractions;
- strong verbs over verb + modifier combinations;
- quantities, examples, and observed details over unsupported intensifiers.

Never invent specificity that is not present in the source.

## Business prose

When a sentence hides responsibility, try to recover:

```text
actor + action + object
```

But retain passive voice when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, intentionally backgrounded, or when the object deserves emphasis.

## Technical prose

Check:

- terminology consistency;
- causal claims;
- assumptions;
- uncertainty;
- distinction between analogy and mechanism;
- whether simplification changed the truth conditions.

## Structure

For every paragraph ask:

1. What job does this paragraph perform?
2. What changed for the reader after reading it?
3. Does it logically follow the previous paragraph?
4. Is there a stronger place for it?
5. Can it be removed without loss?

## Lead

Ask:

- Does the first sentence start the actual piece?
- Is context arriving before there is a reason to care?
- Does sentence one create a reason to read sentence two?

## Ending

Ask:

- Has the piece already ended?
- Is the conclusion only repeating earlier material?
- Does it introduce a new argument too late?
- Is the ending earned rather than announced?

## Voice drift

Compare original and revision for:

- average sentence length;
- vocabulary;
- formality;
- contractions;
- humor;
- directness;
- technical density;
- first-person frequency;
- certainty;
- cadence.

Flag a revision that becomes smoother but less recognizable.

## Synthetic-writing signals

These signals justify inspection, not automatic removal:

- generic openings;
- excessive headings or signposting;
- repeated "not X, but Y" phrasing;
- forced triads;
- empty intensifiers;
- abstract claims of transformation, impact, or innovation;
- generic optimistic conclusions;
- overuse of em dashes;
- uniform sentence lengths;
- polished sentences with no clear owner or point of view.

## Restraint test

If a sentence is already clear, precise, natural, and appropriate to the author's voice, mark it:

**LEAVE UNCHANGED**
